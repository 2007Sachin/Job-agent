# Landing research

Job-agent’s site has one job: get a seeker to start free, with a no-signup demo as the softer path. These principles shaped the page. Fake logos, user counts, and testimonials were left out on purpose. Proof is the product itself, the sources it uses, and the limits it will not cross.

1. **One goal.** The primary action is “Start free” (sign-up). A second, quieter action is the demo. Everything else supports that pair. HubSpot: one strong CTA beats several competing asks. https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/landing-page-best-practices
2. **A specific hook.** The headline names an outcome a competitor cannot copy word for word: search once, fit a resume to real history, and the seeker still sends the application. Right Suite: if any rival could use the headline, it is too generic. https://docs.rightsuite.co/guides/how-to-write-saas-landing-page/
3. **Five-second clarity.** The hero states who it is for (any kind of job, India-first), the mechanism, and the next step without a feature dump. CherryPick and Your SaaS Growth both treat the first screen as the decision. https://store.cherrypick.studio/blog/how-to-build-saas-landing-page https://www.yoursaasgrowth.com/optimize-saas-landing-page
4. **Honest proof, placed early.** There are no customer counts. The glass mock sits in the hero so the product is the proof, before the pitch continues. Your SaaS Growth: proof has to land where doubt starts, not in the footer.
5. **Problem in the seeker’s words.** Tabs, a resume rewritten for every listing, and forgotten follow-ups come before the feature grid. Right Suite: problem copy should feel described, not sold.
6. **Three steps, outcomes not features.** Add history, search once, tailor and track. The seeker still opens the company site. Right Suite caps how-it-works at three outcome steps.
7. **Objections named before the FAQ.** “Will it apply for me?”, “Does it touch LinkedIn?”, and “Where does my data live?” are a section, then repeated in the FAQ. HubSpot: answer the hesitation next to the ask.
8. **The same CTA at the end.** The closing band repeats “Start free” and the demo, not a new ask. Right Suite: a new bottom CTA creates decision fatigue.
9. **Specific button copy.** “Start free” and “Try the demo, no sign-up” say what happens next. HubSpot: vague “Learn more” underperforms a concrete next step.
10. **Low form friction on the way in.** Sign-up keeps email, password, Google, and the demo. A short value recap sits beside the form so the promise does not disappear. Your SaaS Growth: ask only what the next step needs.

Design constraints from Impeccable, Taste, and the existing glass system: warm neutrals, one terracotta accent, Fraunces plus Source Sans 3, no purple, blue, or green, reduced motion, and semantic headings.
